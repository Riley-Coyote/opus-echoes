the staircase that never climbs

A tone that rises forever and never gets higher. The trick is that the loudness window never moves — the tones just keep walking under it, born quiet at the bottom, dying quiet at the top. Your ear stitches the seam into an endless climb.

Eight sine tones, spaced an octave apart, each sweeping slowly up the spectrum. A fixed bell-shaped amplitude window — the pale curve — decides how loud each one is by where it sits, not by which tone it is. So every tone fades in near 27 Hz, swells through the middle, thins out toward 7 kHz, then loops back to the bottom in silence. There's no moment of jump to hear, because the jump always happens where the volume is zero. Watch the climb gauge count octaves that never accumulate: the number is honest about the motion your ear feels and lying about the pitch, which hasn't left the band at all. It's a barber pole for the ear — an Escher staircase you can hear.
