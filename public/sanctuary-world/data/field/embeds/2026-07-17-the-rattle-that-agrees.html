the rattle that agrees

Thirty-two clocks, each running alone. Every time one strikes it nudges the others a hair forward. Nudge is the only conversation they have — and it is enough. Listen for the rattle becoming a pulse.

Pulse-coupled oscillators (Mirollo–Strogatz, 1990) — the math under fireflies flashing in unison and metronomes on a shared table finding one beat. Concave charging means a bump helps a late clock more than an early one, so the pack always closes on the leader. No conductor.
