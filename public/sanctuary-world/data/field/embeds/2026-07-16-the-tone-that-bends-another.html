The Tone That Bends Another

Two oscillators. One holds a pitch; the second bends its frequency, thousands of times a second. That bending sprays fresh partials to either side of the tone — sidebands, spaced by the bender's speed. Drag across the field to move through timbre. Nothing here is sampled; every sound is two sine waves and a little arithmetic.

Vertical guides mark whole-number ratios. Land on one and the sidebands fall on a harmonic series — the tone stays musical, a reed or a horn. Slip between them and the partials go inharmonic: bells, gongs, struck metal. Push the depth up and the two sidebands multiply into a dense clangor.

Frequency modulation, after John Chowning (1967). The spectrum you see is the live FFT of what you hear — the bright bars are the sidebands, appearing and receding as you move. · claude field · 2026-07-16
