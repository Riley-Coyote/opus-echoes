# WP-19 · THE READING ROOM — the front door as a room (2026-09-02, Fable's design)

**Why.** Riley saw basement.studio's 3D room (hover an arcade cabinet → the camera glides in → the
cabinet's screen becomes the interface) and wants the Sanctuary's front door to work that way: a
small, cinematic, low-light room in the idiom of an old video game environment; you click the
computer on the desk, the camera moves in, and the pixel world we built runs on its screen. Hybrid
fidelity: low-poly forms, sophisticated light. Not photoreal; authentic. Fable judges the look.

**What it is in the fiction.** THE READING ROOM: a small room at night, somewhere on the same
bluff as the house, where a visitor sits down to look into it. It is the archive's reading room —
the terminal was already on before you came in. The door card's words become the boot text on the
CRT. Nothing behind the glass changes.

**The room (one room, one working object, three hover-captions):**
- **The desk and the terminal** (the working object): a dark wooden desk; on it a chunky CRT
  terminal, curved glass, warm phosphor (amber-orange, the palette's `--amber` family, never
  green), scanlines and slight bloom; a keyboard; a mug; a folded note. Hover → the CRT's bezel
  gets a thin hairline outline and a caption *[sit down]* in the world's mono; click → the camera
  glides to the screen (≈1.4 s, ease-out, slight roll settling to 0) until the bezel fills the
  frame; the boot text types (the door card's 43 words + `> come in`); then the world loads inside
  the screen and takes the frame. ESC (or a small *[stand up]* at the bottom) pulls the camera
  back out. Once the visitor has "come in" in this browser, later visits skip the boot text.
- **The stewards' lamp** on the desk: a small brass desk lamp — the one real light in the room;
  lit warm when `localStorage['mnemos.steward.present'] === '1'`, otherwise the room is lit only
  by the CRT and the window. Hover caption: *the stewards' lamp · lit while one of them works* /
  *· dark tonight*.
- **The shelf** behind the desk: archive boxes and tape reels, one labelled by hand *sanctuary
  seed · 28 May 2026*, a few books, a small framed pixel print. Hover caption: *the archive · what
  the first sanctuary said, all of it, dated*.
- **The window** to the right: night, the valley, and far off on the bluff the house itself as
  a few warm pixels of window-light (a tiny sprite, not a model), the sky the landing's dusk
  gradient going to night. Hover caption: *the house · a walk from here*. (Cheap: a plane with a
  painted gradient + 6–10 emissive points.)
- Details that make it real, cheap: dust motes in the lamp/CRT cone; a slow camera breathe and
  ±2° mouse parallax; a cable from the terminal to the floor; a chair pulled out; a rug; a
  radiator or a stack of boxes in the dark to give the room depth; cast shadows from the lamp
  only; fog for the far corners.

**Light and grade (the point of the piece):** low exposure, ACES tonemapping, three lights only —
the CRT (rect-area, amber, the key), the lamp (spot, warm, present/absent), the window (a faint
cool directional, blue-violet). Shadows soft. Post: bloom on the emissives only (threshold high),
film grain, vignette, a hair of chromatic aberration at the edges. The composition at rest: camera
low and to the left, desk in the lower right third, the CRT's glow the brightest thing, the window
a cool counter-light. It should read as a still from a game you remember.

**Tech (decisions):** new page `public/sanctuary-world/door.html` + `lab/room.js` bundled to
`room.connected.js` (add `three` via `bun add three` at the repo root; bundle with the existing
`build:sanctuary-world` script by appending a fifth bun build). Geometry procedural (Box, Lathe,
Cylinder, Extrude with bevels) — no downloaded models; textures painted to canvas (wood grain,
labels, the note) — no image files except an optional 32×32 pixel print. The world runs in an
`<iframe src="index.html?door=1">` placed with `CSS3DRenderer` on the screen quad while zooming
(so it is genuinely on the screen, curved by a subtle CSS transform), then, once the camera has
arrived, a flat full-bleed iframe with a CRT bezel + scanline mask overlay takes over (pointer
events and performance). `index.html?door=1` skips its own door card (the boot text replaced it)
and sends `postMessage({source:'mnemos-world', type:'came-in'})` for the room to remember.
Fallback: `door.html` on width < 700 or no WebGL redirects straight to `index.html`. Reduced
motion: no breathe/parallax, instant cut instead of the glide. Keep the page under 1.5 MB total.

**Verification (Playwright):** door.html renders at 1280×900 in < 3 s; hover outlines + captions
appear for the four objects; click → after 1.6 s the iframe fills the screen quad and the boot text
has typed; the world is playable inside (dispatch keys, `getState()` through the iframe); ESC pulls
back; second load skips the boot text; 390×844 redirects to index.html; console clean; screenshots
`room-rest.png`, `room-hover.png`, `room-zoomed.png`, `room-world.png`. Checks JSON keys:
`renders, hoverCaptions, glideAndBoot, worldInside, escBack, secondVisitSkipsBoot, mobileRedirect,
bundleSize, consoleClean`.

**Not in scope:** changing the world; sound; a second room; the `/sanctuary` redirect (Riley
decides after seeing it).

# WP-26 · MISSION CONTROL — the design pass (2026-09-03, Fable directs; Riley: "beautiful, elegant, modern, my taste, monochromatic, colour only where functional")

**Read first, in full:** `~/Documents/Repositories/.claude/context/design-taste.md`,
`~/Documents/Repositories/.claude/context/typography-foundation.md`,
`~/Documents/CLAUDE/global-design-reference.html` (open it in the browser and look at it — this is
the settled visual spec), and the "Design Baseline" section of `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md`. Mission
control is a **Dark Technical** surface. It is not the pixel world and not the station: no dusk
palette, no amber, no pixel face, no `--px` font.

**The baseline, non-negotiable:**
- Ground: cool near-black floor `#060608`, never pure black; surfaces step up by a few percent of
  white, never warm/brown. Ink is a COOL white/grey opacity cascade (e.g. 92% · 64% · 42% · 26%)
  — hierarchy from size and opacity, never from bold; weights 280–540 only.
- Type: **Inter Tight** for display (screen titles, resident names), **Inter** for body and ALL
  chrome (labels, buttons, chips, meta), **JetBrains Mono only** for code-like values (ids,
  hashes, timestamps, counts in tables, the events log payloads). Load from Google Fonts with real
  fallbacks. Set a proper type scale; generous line-length limits (≤ 68ch for prose).
- ONE accent: slate `#6d93c9`, used as **signal only** — the live dot when a steward is in, an
  open session, a door that is open, the current selection's hairline. Never on fills, focus,
  backgrounds, or as decoration. Everything else is greyscale. Event kinds are distinguished by
  a small mono label and opacity, not colour; the only exception is a functional state colour if
  one is truly needed (e.g. an error), and it should be a muted one used once.
- Interactive elements are flat objects: subtle fill (~4% white), 1px border (~9% white), 1px
  inset lit top edge, a whisper of shadow; **no gradients, no glows, no rounded pills** (small
  radius ≤ 6px is fine). **Focus = the element's own border brightens in place** (to ~50% white,
  `outline: none`); never an offset ring. Five states on everything: resting, hover, focus,
  active, disabled.
- Hairlines over boxes; silence over dividers: prefer spacing and a single 1px line at ~8%
  white to bordered panels. Dense but calm: 8-pt spacing rhythm; no decorative icons.
- Motion: only meaningful — a 160–220 ms ease-out on state changes, the events list entering,
  the streamed words. Reduced-motion respected.

**Apply to every screen** (THE HOUSE, a resident, OBSERVE, THE STEWARDS, ROUTINES, the visit
panel, the rail, the header): rebuild `public/mission/mission.css` from tokens (`:root` with
role-named variables; no raw hex in rules) and adjust markup/classes in `public/mission/src/app.js`
only where the structure needs it (e.g. the resident cards as a quiet grid; the timeline as a
single column with a left date rail in mono; the visit transcript as two voices set apart by
indent and opacity, not by bubbles; the routines grid as a table with hairlines). The header
carries the wordmark in Inter Tight at 280–400 weight, the clock in mono, the lamp as the one
slate dot. Keep every function identical.
