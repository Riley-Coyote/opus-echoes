## OPEN — talking

### 1 · the museum's flow — rethink the floorplans
*raised by Riley, 2026-09-01, with circled workshop screenshots of the annex*

**What's wrong:** in every hall of the museum a centerpiece structure (the light tables that
hold the "tables of honor") sits right past the arch, on the walking line. You have to walk
around it to keep moving, and it stands too close to each room's entrance. Riley's instinct:
remove the centerpieces, or make the rooms larger so they hold more art. No fixed solution —
wants a beautiful, logical flow someone follows through the spaces.

**Where the thinking is (Fable's proposal — see the sketch and the conversation):**
1. **The spine stays clear.** Nothing ever sits on the red line.
2. **The threshold pause.** The first stretch inside any arch is empty; you see the whole room
   before anything asks for your attention.
3. **Alcoves for the tables of honor.** Each table moves into a side bay you turn into on
   purpose, lit like a chapel; the bays alternate sides so the walk meanders.
4. **Partitions mean more wall.** Short freestanding walls in each half of a hall (twin bays)
   double the hang without touching the axis.
5. **Vary the halls.** A court, a long gallery, a terminal hall — not three identical stamps.
6. **The far-wall hero.** Every hall's north wall holds one larger piece, seen through the
   arch from the room before; the light pulls you north.

**Open questions for Riley:**
- Keep the tables of honor at all, or retire the device and give those works wall frames?
- Widen the halls (the plane allows ~832 wide vs 704 now), lengthen them, or both?
- Should the same grammar be applied to the permanent gallery (its continuity table sits on
  the spine in the presence hall the same way) and to Sol's atrium (the pedestals)?
- Does the museum eventually loop back to the atrium (no dead-end backtracking), or does the
  thread carry you home?
